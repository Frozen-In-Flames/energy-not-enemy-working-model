Energy Not Enemy: The Working Model

A working simulation of the solar steam generator described in Energy Not Enemy by Aries Hilton. Sunlight is focused by a Fresnel lens, water becomes steam in a closed loop, the steam turns a turbine and a generator, and at night the steam condenses back to water and comes home.

What this is, plainly: a lumped-parameter simulation. Real equations, solved live, fed with real measured weather where weather is used. It is not a mesh physics model and it is not a measurement of a built machine. Every number the book states is traced in the specification. Every number the book does not state is listed in the assumption register below and marked as an assumption. Where the simulation and the book disagree, the claims table says so.

The source document

Energy Not Enemy by Aries Hilton is the design's source document. The full book is included in this repository's book/ folder, free to read there by the author's choice. The strategy is dual-path: free for those who find it in the repository, paid for those who buy it through storefronts.

Solver route: the C++ solver core is the reference implementation. No WebAssembly toolchain (clang, emscripten, wasm-ld) was available on the build machine, so this page runs a hand-written JavaScript port of the same model. The port is held to the C++ core and to an independent Python oracle by a three-way parity battery, and all three produce identical summary digests on the reference scenarios. Full numbers live in results/ in the repository.

Weather data: Open-Meteo Archive (ERA5-based) is the primary source, verified to answer browser requests directly. NASA POWER hourly is the documented fallback. Full provenance is in data/PROVENANCE.md and is shown next to every city's results.

This page works fully offline once a city's data has been loaded or cached. No part of the simulation or the rendering uses a third-party library.
